## 2026-10-14 10:02 — Account-Fair Concurrent Video Processing

### Objective

Implement the approved two-slot video processing plan: allow at most two inference jobs at once, never run two videos from one account at once, and keep queued work durable and claimable across Flask processes.

---

### Summary

Replaced process-local queue authority with SQLite-backed atomic enqueue and worker claims, account-aware round-robin selection, worker leases, persisted cancellation requests, and a two-worker app pool. The GPU reservation supports configurable capacity and is set to two for the shared local inference callers; a default capacity of one remains available to isolated slot instances and existing callers. Focused concurrency, processing, and reservation checks passed. No real-video VRAM benchmark or browser check was performed, and the running Flask process was not restarted.

---

### Detailed Changes

- SQLite `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` makes duplicate enqueue checks and claims atomic across app processes.
- Claims allow a maximum of two running video jobs and exclude accounts that already have a running job. A dispatch cursor prioritizes accounts least recently served so one account's backlog does not monopolize a free slot.
- Worker IDs, heartbeat timestamps, and expiring leases let another process distinguish a live claim from abandoned work. Startup preserves queued jobs and only fails expired or unowned running claims; stale video-level `processing` status is reconciled.
- Queued cancellation is committed in SQLite immediately. Running cancellation is persisted and observed by the worker heartbeat loop, including when the requesting Flask process differs from the worker process.
- Two local worker threads poll the shared DB queue. If a worker cannot reserve a local GPU slot within the bounded wait, it releases the DB claim back to `queued` for retry.
- Existing single-holder slot instances remain compatible; the shared TAVIDM slot admits two reservations and denies further work at capacity.
